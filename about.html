The Journey

My journey is long. Unconventional. Unique. It's a story if nothing else. So if you're under the impression that this page is a summary, well, it's not. There's no way for me to summarize everything that led to where I am today. You'll just have to start here and make to the end, just as I have.

I was told once, in my early twenties, that I had spent most of my life wandering around aimlessly. At the time, I didn't understand. I didn't really think it was true. Truthfully, I didn't give it much thought at all. Little did I know, Ty had never been more right about anything in her entire life than she was when she said this to me.

My journey as an artist goes all the way back to the second grade. I had a fellow classmate, named Cory, who wrote stories about his friends being superheroes. In this class, we never had a "show-and-tell" time. However, Cory was given the time to read his stories to the class every Friday. I became insanely inspired and wrote my first story, using the same idea as his, on construction paper with crayon. I was given the opportunity to read my story aloud like he did. Looking back on it over the years, I'm pretty sure that upset him. Rightfully so. As a single child, I'm confident that all I really wanted at the time was the attention that he was getting from the class and from the teachers. Little did he know how much his actions were going to shape the rest of my life.

This trend of mine continued well into middle school. I used to write stories about my group of friends in a composition notebook. Justin, Cody, Dominique, Natalie, and many others would get the chance to read the first words I put to paper that would become the root of who I am today. By the end of the sixth grade, I had been pushed by my best friend at the time, Ty, and her friend Morgan, to start writing poetry. I was pretty against the idea. At first, it just didn't seem all that interesting to me. Eventually, on my eleventh birthday at the Disney Land Resort, they finally convinced me to write my first poem. Any adult who had read this tiny little poem would have immediately seen where I was headed.

Growing up, my dad had built his own home studio for recording hip-hop music. It was something he was into when he was younger and now that he was a successful adult, it was something he could afford to do. Not to mention he's extremely gifted in everything he decides to do. My friends, Justin and Cody, and myself had a little YouTube channel going. We never really did anything special. There was no real concept to the channel or the videos. But Cody and I used to go to Justin's house to hang out and sometimes Justin would start acting crazy. He would put on the weirdest outfit he could find and come up with a character in his mind. He'd act out an entire scene as Cody and I approached the house. Sometimes he was an old man telling us kids to get off his property, other times he was a sorcerer asking us to choose which path we wish to follow in our own journey. I had the idea of filming these moments and putting them on YouTube. My dad introduced me into Windows Movie Maker. Old right? I know. Version 7 to be precise. The software doesn't even exist anymore, and the native program that now comes with windows is vastly different than what Movie Maker was. In a good way. Oddly enough, this isn't exactly where it all got started. At this point, my journey as an artist had barely even begun.

So. Our channel grew in content. It didn't necessarily grow in views, but, you have to remember that this was the early 2000's. YouTube and social media wasn't what it is today. We weren't trying to go viral. We just wanted to post videos. I myself just wanted to edit videos. At one point in time, my cousin Jay and I did a very similar thing but that didn't last very long. I think I was just as upset as Cory was that Jay wanted to hop on the wagon that my friends and I were on. Whatever the case was, video editing was always there.

Shortly after this, I started using Windows Movie Maker to create lyric videos for my favorite songs. I can't remember which songs I did, but, I'm pretty confident that it likely started with songs from Eminem. Shocking, right? Just wait for what comes next...

One day, when my friends and I still had our channel active, my dad walked into my room as I was editing a video and he said to me 'Hey. You should write a little song for your YouTube channel. Come here. I'll show you how to make a simple little beat and hit record'. That was a huge turning point in my life. I'm still not quite sure if it was for the better, but, it absolutely played a key role in how I got to where I am today.

1.76 million copies sold in the first week of release Sound familiar? It should. This album broke the fastest selling hip-hop record back in the year 2000. Marshal Mathers LP When I found this out, my single child syndrome took over and I became obsessed.

'How come nobody had beat this record, yet?' I asked myself. Keep in mind, it wasn't that I felt Eminem didn't deserve every bit of respect that he got, and then some. To me, by this point in the world, it seemed like it would be easier for someone to break the record with the way the internet was evolving. So I figured 'I guess I'll have to do it myself'. Childish, right? Well. I was a child at the time, in case you've forgotten. I was roughly 14 by this point. I was 14 years old, infected with single child syndrome and had no intentions of letting go of my obsession anytime soon. It wouldn't be until 7-8 years later that I completely left performance arts altogether; and even that wasn't a conscious decision once it happened.

The next decade of my life would define everything I became. I set out with one goal and one goal only: to become the best there ever was and break the record. By 16 years old I was leasing beats from online producers and recording at Undercaste located in Shoreline, WA. The first engineer I worked with at Undercaste taught me how to use my diaphram properly, and how to leverage the idea of character when performing. Forgive me, but I can't remember exactly how to spell his name. However, it was only after two or three sessions with him that he actually ended up moving out of state. Undercaste brought in a new engineer by the name of Andu. Andu and I would spend a few years recording music together and developing an entire character ourselves. I focused on using the skills I developed to truly pin down how this character behaved and Andu spent an entire album learning exactly what I was looking for in the mixing and the sound of the character. For a long time, the name of this 'alter-ego' was simply 'The Junkie'. It was the simplest way I could describe the character we had created for particular moments in any given track. The Junkie first started to be refined on a project titled 'DETOX'; an album I wrote using heroine as a metaphor to describe the abusive relationship I had with Tatiana Boskofsky.

I won't go into details about my entire relatiobnship with Tatiana, the devil's spawn. However, I will say that I was raised right, it's against my very nature to hit women and even when I would try to defend myself, her entire family would come in on the abuse. I remember one time I found myself on the floor of her bedroom. Her, her brother and her father were all kicking my ribs in at the same time. It was a brutal time of my life and ended just before the two year mark. This was between the ages of 20 and 22, roughly. I finally left Tatiana when I found myself crawling out of the house and across the lawn, my entire face soaked in blood after she threw an inch-thick ashtray at my head as I was restraining her brother from attacking me. My dad used to tell me 'luck is when opportunity meets preparation'. If that's the case, then my survival was merely by chance. The neighbor across the street happened to be mowing his lawn. I made it across Tatiana's yard before he happened to turn and face my way. He and his wife helped me up, called an ambulance and I made it to the hospital. I called my mom from their phone, naively assuming that the hospital would call her and I didn't want her to hear what had happened from them. Instinctively, my amazing and wonderful mother headed straight to the hospital with no hesitation. I'm sure she got a couple speeding tickets along the way, too. Worried about what would happen if she saw the result of the fight, I spent the entire time in the bathroom trying to wash all of the blood off of my face. She was devestated when she saw me, immediately in tears. To this day, she has no idea how much I was able to wash off in that restroom.

My father is a King. He is the most respectable and talented man I have ever met in my life. Some of the best friends I ever had, people like Levon and PJ, often reminded me of him in their own ways. I think that's why I admired them so much. If there is a goal to be achieved, my dad will achieve it. I'm sure many people who don't really know my dad might assume my dad's main focus is money. I believed that for some time as well. However, that's never really been the case. My dad once told me that the thing that really motivates him is accomplishments. From my perspective, the discipline it takes to earn an Associate's Degree is a far better feeling than the money he can make with whatever career he decides to pursue with the degree. With that being said, my father and I are very different people. Yet, we're also very similar. This odd combination makes it extremely difficult for us to get along. I'm am much more quick to jump to anger than he is. My father may yell and get upset, but, my father will not lay his hands on me. He won't really lay his hands on anyone for that matter. My father is extremely against violence and this goes back years before I was ever born. I remember growing up, my dad refused to spank me when I would act out of line. He was much more the "ground him and take away his privileges" type. The only time I ever remember my dad attempting to spank me, it didn't even hurt. It made him extremely uncomfortable to even do that. He is loyal, loving and compassionate, despite any flaws he may have just for being a human being; because we all do. For that, I truly love him. Even if we don't always get along.

After my time with Tatiana and experimenting with psychadelics, I moved out to a nearby city, Lake Stevens. I had burned every bridge I had ever built. Manon, who I had done music with for a long time; Sergio, who had been like a brother to me since elementary school; Levon, who had been like a mentor to me as I became an adult; PJ, who had is still the best friend I ever had; my uncle James, who gave me a place to stay shortly after my relationship with Tatiana; and of course my parents. I had found myself reconnecting with a friend from middle school. Someone I won't mention simply because my time in Lake Stevens is an entire story all on its own. This friend of mine, and the family we had all created while out there, were there for me in ways I hadn't realized as it was happening. It was in Lake Stevens when I began to get my head straight and get away from all of the noise in the city. It's also when I began to wonder whether or not I even wanted to continue pursuing a music career at all. I reached out to Levon and told him I think it was about time I put it all to rest. I didn't really see a point. I'm not sure what all was going through my mind at the time I reached out, but, I'm pretty sure I was getting bored of the routine. Write a story, break that story into 7-10 hip-hop songs, record and create an album and release it to the public. Then do it all over again the next year. From my perspective, if anything was going to come out of this 'career', I would have seen a sign by then. Levon was furious. Instantly. He gave me a lecture about how I was one of the most motivated, young artists he ever knew. That despite him being several years older than me, I inspired him in a couple ways as an artist and producer himself. I was a little caught off guard to find out how much he would be disappointed if I had given up on my pursuit. I wasn't entirely motivated to keep going, but, I hadn't consciously decided to quit either. It's a good thing I didn't.

At some point while I was in Lake Stevens, I had started working at a local shop and met a good friend named Ryan. Believe me when I tell you, Ryan is the absolute best artist to ever exist on the planet. I remember telling people 'Ryan is the next'. Sounds weird, right? Next what? That was the thing. He wasn't the next Linkin Park or the next Michael Jackson. Ryan was his own entity. Something I had never heard before. Ryan was an entirely new wave of talent that I felt nobody could compare to. As long as he gave himself a shot, Ryan was going to be the next biggest artist of all time. One day, while sitting outside on the property with the others, a couple of lyrics came to my mind. Just two bars. Small. No real concept had formed quite yet. But it was the first real spark as an artist I had felt in a few years. So I ran with it. I wrote a song called 'Searchin', and when I got to the hook, all I could imagine was Ryan on it. In less than two weeks I was back at Undercaste with Andu laying down the verses. I brought it to Ryan and he fell in love with the idea almost instantly. That's when things really started to come together for me as an artist.

Within the next year or so, the life we all had started to build for ourselves out in Lake Stevens started to shift in ways that were out our control. Each of us moved out and off of the property. I found myself in a little room I was renting in my hometown, Everett, Washington. Most of the others were living down in Shoreline. The one friend who was the glue for all of us, excluding Ryan, had passed away from an overdose. It struck us all extremely hard. This was the first time I had experienced a death of a loved one in my entire life and at the same time, Ryan had relapsed and was letting his life fall apart.

In the year 2020, at the start of the pandemic, I wrote the darkest album I have ever written. The project was initially called 'Broken Clock' and overtime it became 'Product of the Curse'. With two major tracks 'Truth Hurtz' and 'Ghost', the album was something I held very close to me. I had developed a concept of a trilogy for the entire story. First I would release an album called 'Product of the Curse' where most songs described the time I spent on the streets after leaving my parents house at 18, and a few songs describing the assault that broke my family apart. The second album was going to be called 'The Stay Safe Project', an album designed to inspire and give hope to addicts in recovery. The third album, which I never really got around to writing, would be the end of the entire story altogether. The larger idea was inspired by the few tracks I had recorded with Ryan. Mostly, 'Follow The Light Part Two'. As Ryan's substance abuse became worse over the months, I wrote a song called Stay Safe where Andu encouraged me to sing the hook myself. I've never been much of a singer, however, since the track was a tribute to Ryan and my way of telling him I wish him the best, I couldn't ignore Andu. He was right. I couldn't let anyone else do the hook, I had to do it myself. And it turned out to be one of the best songs I ever recorded and the main inspiration behind the sequel project. Ryan entered a rehabilitation program that was supposed to be completed by the end of the year. So I gave myself a goal. I would finish everything for Product of the Curse on my own, build a buzz, and get to work on our collab album "Stay Safe" for when he finished the program. This is when the artist in me had really started to flourish.

When recording the song 'Ghost' awhile back, I had a particular way of ending the song. Unlike most songs I wrote, this song didn't have a hook. It was simply the story from start to finish, uninterrupted. At the very end of the song I ended the vocals with a unqiue was of saying 'Ghost'. At the time, my vocal 'signature' was to simply say "CursedMuzick". Nothing special. It was my stage name, there was no special mixing or vocal technique to it. Just an average and common way of getting one's name out there through the audio. However, one of my favorite rappers, NF, had already done it in a similar fashion for his whole career and brand. So I started to get a little nervous about the idea that I was just copying him. I wrote dark and edgy music, like Eminem, NF and Ekoh, and I had the term "music" in my name, like NF. So I began experimenting with the word "Cursed" alone. I used my skills from developing 'The Junkie' to enhance the creative aspect of it and very quickly I had a vocal signature I was very proud of. So much so, Andu had a specific was of mixing it. The signature was largely inspired by the way I ended the song 'Ghost' and Ghost was the number one most important track on Product of the Curse. This made it feel very natural to cling to and I have yet to move on from it.

This vocal signature immediately became the very essence of the alter-ego I had developed over the years. It wasn't just a signature. It was The Junkie announcing his presence both on the song and in my mind as I was writing the tracks. With the album finished and this alter-ego completely developed, so much so I was able to bounce back and forth between my natural performance vocals and 'Cursed' in a single recording without punching in, I set out to create a video that could match the quality of the album. That's where things started to go wrong, even though I didn't realize it at the time.

Product of the Curse, from the very beginning, ever since I wrote 'Searchin', was too heavy and too raw for an average music video. Even a music video for the three main tracks weren't going to be enough. The moment I had all of the pieces to the idea together, I knew that the only way I was going to be able to properly tell this story was to create a short film. A dark, edgy, horror-style short-film using Product of the Curse as the soundtrack. I had played around with this general concept years ago when I wrote DETOX. However, I had used PayPal to make the payment to the videographer and I was coming across issues because of it. Once the payment and issues had cleared away, we both moved on from the project. He sent me a refund and I moved on with my life. This time, I was determined. I began searching for local videographers for hire that would be willing to come out to Everett to film. It took a little while, but I found one who's instagram tag was 'KGRMajesty'. Sound familiar? He's the gentleman in the video on the homepage of this website. Check that interview out to learn more about him and his story.

Kaeden, or KG as I called him, was a highly talented solo shooter. This would be the first inspiration behind the idea of me getting into visual arts. When I reached out to KG, I was worried. Not because of him or his portfolio. His work was phenomenal and his pricing was highly affordable given the product that would be delivered. I was simply worried that whoever I hired wouldn't be willing to spend the time I needed to make this short-film the way I needed. And to be fair, I didn't quite realizing what I was asking for until years later. I told myself I should do a few trial runs with KG before I gave him the main project. At the same time, I was just started to get into selling merchandise as an artist. So to have someone on the team who could handle the video work while I focused on my craft and merch, I felt I was in a very solid position. And KG did a marvelous job for that first video.

The first video we did together was titled "Picture Perfect Goalz". As I'm sure you recall, my father and I don't have a very good relationship. At this time in my life we hardly spoke at all. Yet, when he caught wind of the song and saw the video, he reached out to me on instagram and told me he would promote it for $1000, asking for absolutely nothing in return. I immediately felt conflicting and extreme emotions about the whole idea. For one, the track wasn't even a whole entire song. It was a single verse I wrote for fun one day while rewatching "How I Met Your Mother". The first two bars of the track were "I need a penthouse up in NYC and a 300-inch TV screen". This is literally what Barney has in the show. The entire verse was Barney's life with a beat slapped to it. Secondly, the song was essentially a mainstream style, radio-hit like song. It was nowhere near the style of music I intended to continue making; and that's what bothered me the most. If my dad had promoted the song and I grew a buzz from it, I'd have absolutely no follow-up. The type of fanbase I'd generate from the song wouldn't be interested in music I continued to create. At the same time, I have only ever been the second worst critic when it came to my music. My dad has always been the worst, and that's a good thing. My dad has always pushed me to be a better artist, even if he wasn't interested in me pursuing a music career. When I was younger my dad used to say "it does not matter how good a song is. As long as it's better than the last one you released". So for Picture Perfect Goalz to catch his attention so well that he was willing to spend a grand on the song? That to this day is one of my greatest accomplishments

Suffice to say, because of my reaction to his offer, we did not promote the song. I continued forward with KG and releasing music that was more aligned with the Cursed Brand. More aligned with mental health awareness and addiction recovery. More aligned with Product of the Curse. By the end of the year, KG and I had completed four projects together. And by the end of the fourth video, I started to realize that in order for me to get the short-film made the way I needed to, I was going to have to do it myself. 2021 was the drastic change in direction in regards to my entire life.

In Febuary of 2021, I found myself working at a shop managed by the best manager I ever had, Riley. It was under Riley's management that I was able to pursue my passion and my goals. While working for Riley I was able to save up enough money to buy my first Macbook Pro and begin my journey as a digital artist. It was also under her management that I met my best friend Xander. Xander, just a few months ago in the year 2026, finsihed his Bachelor's in 3D Animation. The day he walked into the shop all those years ago, the very first time I ever spoke to him, I asked him "so what do you work on? Windows or Mac?". It was a trick question. Most people have a strong preference, whether they are a programmer, artist, or gamer. Whatever the case, most people are extremely passionate about the one they choose and why. I myself, being raised by a Windows-favoring software engineer and an Apple-loving queen, understood that they both were great. What was Xander's response? "I use both". I immediately stuck my hand out and said "we're going to be great friends, my guy." And he just laughed.
